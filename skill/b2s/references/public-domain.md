# Public-domain guide (general information, not legal advice)

## Contents
- Quick rules
- Watch-outs
- Safe starting stories
- Redirect ideas for popular modern requests

## Quick rules

- **US:** a work published in 1930 or earlier is public domain (as of 2026). Other countries often use the author's life plus 70 years, so mention that markets differ.
- **Copyright protects expression, not plot or themes.** Even for a protected work, ideas and themes are free, but retelling its specific characters and plot closely is risky. Treat protected works as off-limits and redirect.
- **Historical events are facts and are not copyrightable.** Do not borrow invented characters, scenes, dialogue, or songs from films or books about them.
- **Record the source.** Always fill the `source` block with the edition used and the public-domain basis, and leave `human_verified` false.

## Watch-outs

- **Modern adaptations:** Disney versions of Alice, Pinocchio, Snow White, and Winnie-the-Pooh (many designs and songs), film and TV versions of Sherlock Holmes, Robin Hood, and Romeo and Juliet (for example West Side Story) stay protected even though the books are not. Base songs on the original text and plot only.
- **Phrases from adaptations:** "Elementary, my dear Watson" comes from later adaptations, not Doyle. Avoid it, along with other famous film lines.
- **Translations:** modern English translations of Grimm, Andersen, Collodi, Dumas, and others can carry their own copyright. Write from the plot, never from a translation's wording.
- **Retellings and illustrations:** illustrated editions and modern retellings can be protected. Do not copy their added details.
- **Peter Pan in the UK:** a special law gives a children's hospital a perpetual royalty. Flag it if the user plans to release in the UK.
- **Versions that differ:** Grimm and Perrault differ (for example Little Red Riding Hood, where Perrault ends with no rescue). Pick one version and name it in `source.edition`.
- **Self-harm and tragedy:** Romeo and Juliet and similar stories are public domain, but platforms apply their own self-harm and suicide policies. Keep such songs non-graphic, with no method details, and suggest a human review before posting.

## Safe starting stories (US public domain)

- **Fairy tales and folk tales:** Cinderella, Sleeping Beauty, Puss in Boots, Hansel and Gretel, Snow White, Rapunzel, Little Red Riding Hood, Goldilocks and the Three Bears, The Little Mermaid, The Ugly Duckling, The Snow Queen, Three Little Pigs, Jack and the Beanstalk, Aladdin, The Pied Piper.
- **Children's classics:** Peter Pan, The Wizard of Oz, The Jungle Book, Alice in Wonderland, Through the Looking-Glass, Pinocchio, The Tale of Peter Rabbit, The Wind in the Willows, The Secret Garden, Heidi, Anne of Green Gables, Little Women, Black Beauty, Winnie-the-Pooh (1926), Pollyanna.
- **Adventure and classics:** Treasure Island, Kidnapped, The Call of the Wild, White Fang, Robinson Crusoe, Gulliver's Travels, Moby-Dick, The Count of Monte Cristo, The Three Musketeers, Around the World in 80 Days, Twenty Thousand Leagues Under the Sea, Tom Sawyer, Huckleberry Finn (handle language with care), A Christmas Carol, Sherlock Holmes stories, Dracula, Jekyll and Hyde, The Time Machine, The War of the Worlds, Robin Hood (Pyle, 1883).
- **Myth and drama:** The Odyssey, The Iliad, Beowulf, King Arthur legends, Shakespeare's plays, Greek and Norse myths.
- **Literary novels:** Pride and Prejudice, Persuasion, Jane Eyre, Wuthering Heights, Great Expectations, Frankenstein, Les Miserables, The Great Gatsby (1925), The Metamorphosis.

Many of the darker works need the general channel, or heavy softening for the kids channel.

## Redirect ideas for popular modern requests

Use these as starting points when a user asks about a protected work. The goal is a public-domain story with the same appeal, not a disguised copy.

- A love that waits, lost-and-found romance: Persuasion, Pride and Prejudice, Jane Eyre.
- A ship, disaster, or survival story: the real history of the event, or Treasure Island or Robinson Crusoe.
- A wizard school or young hero's quest: Alice in Wonderland, The Wizard of Oz, Peter Pan, Greek myths.
- A rebellion against a harsh ruler: Robin Hood, King Arthur legends.
- A mystery: Sherlock Holmes stories.
- A kids' adventure with animals: The Jungle Book, The Wind in the Willows, The Tale of Peter Rabbit.

Never copy a protected work's characters, plot beats, signature lines, or songs while redirecting.
